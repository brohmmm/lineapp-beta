      FAQ.Go on. Ask the awkward ones.

      Ask yours in the form.

      "We've had festival apps before. Nobody used them, and they died on Monday."

      Then you know what to ask. We agree an adoption target with you before launch, put QR codes at the gates and show you installs every day. At the example festival, 52 % of guests opened the app. If the numbers are bad, the Monday report says so in its first line.

      "I don't want people with their noses in their phones."

      Neither do we. The app gives the answer in two taps and gets out of the way: who's on, where, how far. Fewer minutes on screen, not more.

      "And in a storm, what about everyone who hasn't got the app?"

      The app is one more channel, not your safety plan. Your PA, screens and stewards still lead. The push reaches the phones a speaker can't: the far bar, the campsite, the tram stop. We write the alert templates with your safety lead before the gates open.

      "This must be hard to run. We have no IT team."

      You don't need one. You hand us a poster and a running order; the panel is as simple as a form. Press Send and it's on every phone with the app.

      "How do I know people actually saw my logo?"

      Every time it's on screen, it's counted. The report shows how many people saw it and how many times, place by place. At the example festival: 3,100 people, 248,000 times.

      "Can we get the contacts of people who visit our zone?"

      Yes, if they say yes. A guest scans the code in your zone, reads your name and the reason, and switches it on. You get a first name and a number with the consent recorded. It's never pre-ticked, and one tap takes it back.

      "We're an alcohol brand. Can we even do this?"

      Yes, with rules built in: pushes only to guests past the 18+ check, at sensible hours, three a day at most. Copy about music and good nights, not about drinking more. Rules per market to confirm.

      "Will my competitor be in the same app?"

      Not in your category: one sponsor per category per edition. You approve every cover, card and push before it runs, and the festival can pause sponsor content at any moment, as it does in a storm. Rule to confirm.

      "Sponsors won't pay extra."

      It doesn't have to be extra: it comes out of the activation budget they already spend at your festival, and it's the first thing you sell them that comes with proof. We write the package, the deck and the report; you make the introduction.

      "What does it cost?"

      £4,000 for one edition at a festival of up to 10,000. One sponsor package of £8,000 pays for it and pays you the same again. If no package sells, you pay the £4,000 yourself.

      "Who pays whom?"

      The sponsor pays the festival, as with any sponsorship. The festival pays LineApp a fixed £4,000 for the app. The sponsor's money pays for the app first; everything above £4,000 stays with the festival. At the full £8,000 package that is half and half. Model to confirm.

      "An app that lives for two days is expensive."

      It works before (announcements, early bird), during (My plan, map, alerts) and after (the report, the next edition). With a package sold, the sponsor's money covers the app first, and everything above £4,000 stays with you.

      "How long does it take?"

      A line-up, a map and your brand book are enough to start. Your branded demo lands within 24 hours of the form below.

      Who owns the data: us or you?

      You do. Your festival is the controller; LineApp is the processor, under a data processing agreement signed before launch. Leave whenever you like and take everything in one export, free. Contract terms to confirm.

      What do festival-goers have to give up?

      Very little. No account needed; analytics anonymous by default; location off until switched on; a plain-English consent screen for each purpose. People see and delete what we hold in two taps.

      Our ticketing partner already holds our audience. What's different?

      Your ticketing tells you how many tickets you sold, not who came. Connected, the app adds who paid and who came, gate by gate. It's your festival's data; we don't keep a copy for ourselves.

      How long do you keep it?

      You choose the retention; by default personal data is deleted after your festival's agreed period (to confirm) and anonymous totals stay.

      "What if it crashes at the worst possible moment?"

      It was built for the worst possible moment. The line-up, My plan and the map work offline, and the push doesn't need social media. People on call all weekend (support hours to confirm).

      Can we take the data into our own tools?

      Yes. CSV or Google Sheets, opted-in contacts to Mailchimp or Brevo, or an API and webhooks. No export fees. Integrations to confirm.

      What needs an integration, and what works on its own?

      On its own: app users, pushes and read rates, the map, sponsor places and contacts with consent. With your ticketing: who paid, and inside vs outside. With your bar tills: the shortest queue. With your locker partner: lockers taken.

      "Does my crew see where I am all the time?"

      Only if you share it, only with your crew, and you can switch it off in two taps.

      "Do I need an account?"

      No. Open the app and go. An emoji is enough to be someone for the weekend.
